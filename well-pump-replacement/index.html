Well Pump Replacement: Cost, Signs, and Lifespan

A well pump replacement is the single biggest routine expense of owning a well — and the most predictable. Submersible pumps in Central Florida typically last 10 to 15 years, and the average replacement lands around $1,900, covering the pump, labor to pull and reset it, and basic electrical and disinfection work.

The mistake to avoid is replacing blind. A failing pressure tank can mimic a dying pump, and a $150 pressure switch can mimic both. A proper quote starts with diagnosis, then itemizes pump, tank, and controls separately so you only buy what actually failed.

  • Failure signs: cycling, pressure loss, air, sand, breaker trips

Common questions

What is the average well pump replacement cost?

Around $1,900 in Central Florida for a standard submersible pump. Shallow jet pumps cost less; deep, high-horsepower, or tank-included jobs cost more.

How long should a well pump last?

Plan on 10–15 years. Frequent cycling, a waterlogged tank, and heavy irrigation use are the top lifespan killers — all fixable once spotted.
